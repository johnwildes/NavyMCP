# EA-18G Growler

**Type:** Carrier-based electronic warfare aircraft
**Manufacturer:** Boeing
**First Flight:** August 2006
**Introduction to Service:** September 2009
**Status:** Active

## Specifications
- **Crew:** 2 (pilot and electronic warfare officer)
- **Length:** 60 ft 1.25 in (18.31 m)
- **Wingspan:** 44 ft 8.5 in (13.62 m)
- **Height:** 16 ft (4.88 m)
- **Wing Area:** 500 sq ft (46.5 sq m)
- **Empty Weight:** 33,094 lb (15,011 kg)
- **Max Takeoff Weight:** 66,000 lb (29,937 kg)
- **Powerplant:** 2 × General Electric F414-GE-400 turbofans, 22,000 lbf (98 kN) thrust each with afterburner

## Performance
- **Maximum Speed:** Mach 1.8 (1,190 mph, 1,915 km/h)
- **Combat Radius:** 390 nmi (449 mi, 722 km)
- **Service Ceiling:** 50,000+ ft (15,000+ m)
- **Rate of Climb:** 44,882 ft/min (228 m/s)

## Electronic Warfare Systems
- **AN/ALQ-218 Tactical Jamming Receiver:** Advanced radio frequency detection system
- **AN/ALQ-227 Communication Countermeasures Set:** Communications jamming system
- **AN/ALQ-99 Tactical Jamming Pods:** High-powered jamming pods (legacy system being phased out)
- **AN/ALQ-249 Next Generation Jammer (NGJ):** Advanced electronic attack system replacing the ALQ-99 (Mid-band pods)
- **AN/ALQ-249(V)2 NGJ-LB:** Low-band jamming pods
- **AN/ALQ-249(V)3 NGJ-HB:** High-band jamming pods
- **AN/APG-79 AESA Radar:** Active electronically scanned array radar system
- **INCANS (Interference Cancellation System):** Allows simultaneous receive and transmit capabilities

## Armament
- **Air-to-Air Missiles:** 2× AIM-120 AMRAAM
- **Anti-radiation Missiles:** AGM-88 HARM, AGM-88E AARGM, AGM-88G AARGM-ER
- **Self-defense:** AIM-9 Sidewinder missiles

## Mission
The EA-18G Growler is a specialized electronic attack variant of the F/A-18F Super Hornet. Primary missions include:

- Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses (SEAD)
- Electronic Attack (EA)
- Electronic Surveillance
- Communication Jamming
- Radar Jamming
- Electronic Protection
- Command and Control Warfare

## History
The EA-18G Growler is the replacement for the EA-6B Prowler and combines the proven F/A-18F Super Hornet platform with advanced electronic warfare capabilities. It entered service in 2009 and achieved full operational capability in 2012. The Growler has been deployed in numerous operations, providing electronic warfare support for various missions. It is the primary electronic warfare platform for the U.S. Navy and Marine Corps, with continued upgrades planned to keep pace with evolving electronic threats. Australia is the only foreign operator of the EA-18G Growler.
