# USS William P. Lawrence (DDG-110)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight IIA)
**Namesake:** Vice Admiral William P. Lawrence
**Commissioned:** June 4, 2011
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Base San Diego, California

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 9,200 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 509 ft (155 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/62 caliber Mk 45 gun
- 2 × 25 mm Mk 38 chain guns
- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 2 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 2 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopters

## History
USS William P. Lawrence is the sixtieth ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for Vice Admiral William P. Lawrence, a naval aviator, test pilot, and Vietnam War prisoner of war who was held captive for six years. During his captivity, he helped develop the POW code of conduct known as the "tap code," which allowed prisoners to communicate with each other.

The ship was commissioned on June 4, 2011, and has participated in numerous operations throughout the Pacific and Middle East. USS William P. Lawrence has conducted freedom of navigation operations in the South China Sea and has been deployed as part of carrier strike groups for regional security operations. In 2016, the ship made history when it sailed within 12 nautical miles of Fiery Cross Reef in the disputed Spratly Islands.
