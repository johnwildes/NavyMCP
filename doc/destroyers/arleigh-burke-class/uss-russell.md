# USS Russell (DDG-59)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight I)
**Namesake:** Rear Admiral John Henry Russell and Admiral James Sargent Russell
**Commissioned:** May 20, 1995
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Base San Diego, California

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 8,230 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 505 ft (154 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/54 caliber Mk 45 gun
- 2 × 25 mm Mk 38 chain guns
- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 1 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 1 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopter

## History
USS Russell is the ninth ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for two distinguished naval officers from the same family: Rear Admiral John Henry Russell (1827-1897) and his son, Admiral James Sargent Russell (1903-1996). The elder Russell served during the Civil War, while the younger Russell was a naval aviator who later served as Vice Chief of Naval Operations.

The ship was commissioned on May 20, 1995, and was formerly homeported at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii before being reassigned to San Diego. USS Russell has participated in numerous operations including deployments to the Western Pacific and the Persian Gulf in support of Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Iraqi Freedom.
