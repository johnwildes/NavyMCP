# USS Pinckney (DDG-91)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight IIA)
**Namesake:** Cook First Class William Pinckney
**Commissioned:** May 29, 2004
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Base San Diego, California

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 9,200 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 509 ft (155 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/62 caliber Mk 45 gun
- 2 × 25 mm Mk 38 chain guns
- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 2 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 2 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopters

## History
USS Pinckney is the forty-first ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for Cook First Class William Pinckney, who received the Navy Cross for his heroism aboard the USS Enterprise during the Battle of Santa Cruz Islands in World War II. When an explosion killed four of his shipmates and severely wounded another, Pinckney carried the injured sailor to safety despite the hazardous fire and smoke.

The ship was commissioned on May 29, 2004, and has participated in numerous operations including counter-narcotics patrols in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific. In 2014, USS Pinckney was involved in the international search effort for Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 in the South China Sea and Indian Ocean.
