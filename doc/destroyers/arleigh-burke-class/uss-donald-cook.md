# USS Donald Cook (DDG-75)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight I)
**Namesake:** Colonel Donald Cook
**Commissioned:** December 4, 1998
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Station Mayport, Florida

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 8,230 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 505 ft (154 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/54 caliber Mk 45 gun
- 2 × 25 mm Mk 38 chain guns
- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 1 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 1 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopter

## History
USS Donald Cook is the twenty-fifth ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for Colonel Donald Cook, a Marine Corps officer who was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor for his heroism as a prisoner of war in Vietnam from 1964 until his death in captivity in 1967.

The ship was commissioned on December 4, 1998, and was formerly forward-deployed to Naval Station Rota, Spain as part of NATO's ballistic missile defense shield in Europe. USS Donald Cook has participated in numerous operations and has been involved in several high-profile encounters with Russian military aircraft while operating in the Black Sea and Baltic Sea regions.
