# USS Ross (DDG-71)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight I)
**Namesake:** Captain Donald K. Ross
**Commissioned:** June 28, 1997
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Station Rota, Spain

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 8,230 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 505 ft (154 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/54 caliber Mk 45 gun
- 2 × 25 mm Mk 38 chain guns
- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 1 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 1 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopter

## History
USS Ross is the twenty-first ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for Captain Donald K. Ross, who received the Medal of Honor for his actions during the attack on Pearl Harbor while serving aboard USS Nevada. Despite being temporarily blinded and severely wounded, he continued to perform his duties.

The ship was commissioned on June 28, 1997, and is forward-deployed to Naval Station Rota, Spain as part of NATO's ballistic missile defense shield in Europe. USS Ross has participated in numerous operations including launching Tomahawk cruise missiles against targets in Syria in 2017.
