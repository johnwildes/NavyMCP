# USS Porter (DDG-78)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight II)
**Namesake:** Commodore David Porter and Admiral David Dixon Porter
**Commissioned:** March 20, 1999
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Station Rota, Spain

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 8,900 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 505 ft (154 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/54 caliber Mk 45 gun
- 2 × 25 mm Mk 38 chain guns
- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 1 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 1 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopter

## History
USS Porter is the twenty-eighth ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for two naval heroes: Commodore David Porter (1780–1843), who served in the War of 1812 and the Barbary Wars, and his son, Admiral David Dixon Porter (1813–1891), who served with distinction in the American Civil War.

The ship was commissioned on March 20, 1999, and is forward-deployed to Naval Station Rota, Spain as part of NATO's ballistic missile defense shield in Europe. In 2012, USS Porter was involved in a collision with a Japanese-owned oil tanker near the Strait of Hormuz, but was repaired and returned to service.
