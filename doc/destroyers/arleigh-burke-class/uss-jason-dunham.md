# USS Jason Dunham (DDG-109)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight IIA)
**Namesake:** Corporal Jason Dunham
**Commissioned:** November 13, 2010
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 9,200 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 509 ft (155 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament

- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 2 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopters

## History
USS Jason Dunham is the fifty-ninth ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers. She is named for Corporal Jason Dunham, a United States Marine who was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor for his actions in Iraq in 2004. When his patrol was ambushed, Dunham deliberately covered an enemy grenade with his helmet and body to save the lives of fellow Marines, sacrificing himself in the process.

The ship was commissioned on November 13, 2010, and has participated in numerous operations including deployments to the Mediterranean Sea and the Middle East. USS Jason Dunham conducts maritime security operations, ballistic missile defense, and multinational exercises with allied navies. In 2018, the ship made headlines when its crew seized over 1,000 AK-47 rifles from a stateless vessel in the Gulf of Aden.
