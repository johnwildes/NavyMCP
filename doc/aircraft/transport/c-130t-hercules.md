# C-130T Hercules

**Type:** Medium-range tactical transport aircraft
**Manufacturer:** Lockheed Martin
**First Flight:** August 23, 1954 (C-130 prototype)
**Introduction to Service:** 1991 (C-130T variant)
**Status:** Active

## Variants
- **C-130T**: U.S. Navy Reserve transport variant
- **KC-130T**: Aerial refueling variant (Marine Corps)

## Specifications
- **Crew:** 5 (2 pilots, flight engineer, navigator, loadmaster)
- **Passenger Capacity:** Up to 92 troops, 64 paratroopers, or 74 litter patients
- **Cargo Capacity:** 45,000 lb (20,412 kg) of cargo
- **Length:** 97 ft 9 in (29.8 m)
- **Wingspan:** 132 ft 7 in (40.4 m)
- **Height:** 38 ft 3 in (11.6 m)
- **Empty Weight:** 75,800 lb (34,400 kg)
- **Max Takeoff Weight:** 155,000 lb (70,307 kg)
- **Powerplant:** 4 × Allison T56-A-15 turboprop engines, 4,590 shp (3,430 kW) each

## Performance
- **Maximum Speed:** 320 knots (368 mph, 593 km/h)
- **Cruise Speed:** 292 knots (336 mph, 541 km/h)
- **Range:** 2,050 nmi (2,360 mi, 3,800 km) with maximum payload
- **Ferry Range:** 4,250 nmi (4,900 mi, 7,890 km)
- **Service Ceiling:** 33,000 ft (10,000 m)
- **Rate of Climb:** 1,900 ft/min (9.7 m/s)

## Fleet Information
- **Inventory:** Approximately 20 in active service with Navy Reserve (as of 2025)
- **Unit Cost:** $75 million (replacement cost)

## Mission
The C-130T Hercules serves as a versatile medium-range tactical transport for the U.S. Navy Reserve. Its missions include:

- Medium-range logistics support
- Personnel transport
- Cargo delivery
- Humanitarian assistance and disaster relief
- Fleet logistics support
- Antarctic support operations (Operation Deep Freeze)
- Search and rescue support
- Aeromedical evacuation

## Features
- **Rear Loading Ramp:** For efficient loading/unloading of vehicles, cargo, and personnel
- **Unimproved Runway Capability:** Can operate from short, unprepared airfields
- **Versatile Cargo System:** Palletized cargo system for quick configuration changes
- **Aerial Delivery Systems:** Capable of airdrop operations
- **Navigation Systems:** Modern avionics suite with GPS and inertial navigation
- **Self-Contained Loading System:** Built-in winch for loading cargo
- **Defensive Systems:** Basic countermeasures for operational security
- **Tactical Systems:** Night vision compatible lighting and operations equipment

## History
While the C-130 Hercules first flew in 1954, the U.S. Navy acquired the C-130T variant in the early 1990s to replace aging C-130F/G models in Navy Reserve squadrons. The C-130T represented a significant upgrade in avionics and systems reliability from earlier models. Throughout its service, the C-130T has supported fleet logistics requirements worldwide, participated in humanitarian assistance missions, and provided critical transportation during emergency response efforts. The aircraft's versatility and reliability have made it a mainstay of the Navy's logistics capabilities. The Navy continues to upgrade its C-130T fleet with modern avionics and systems to extend service life, with plans to keep the aircraft flying well into the 2030s.
