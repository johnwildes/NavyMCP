# P-3C Orion

**Type:** Maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare aircraft
**Manufacturer:** Lockheed Corporation
**First Flight:** November 25, 1959 (P-3 prototype)
**Introduction to Service:** 1962 (P-3A), 1969 (P-3C)
**Status:** Limited active service, being phased out

## Variants
- **P-3C**: Primary U.S. Navy variant with updated avionics and weapons systems
- **P-3C AIP**: Anti-surface warfare Improvement Program variant
- **EP-3E**: Electronic intelligence variant

## Specifications
- **Crew:** 11 (3 pilots, 2 flight engineers, 6 naval flight officers/technicians)
- **Length:** 116 ft 10 in (35.61 m)
- **Wingspan:** 99 ft 8 in (30.37 m)
- **Height:** 33 ft 8.5 in (10.27 m)
- **Empty Weight:** 61,491 lb (27,892 kg)
- **Max Takeoff Weight:** 142,000 lb (64,410 kg)
- **Powerplant:** 4 × Allison T56-A-14 turboprop engines, 4,600 shp (3,430 kW) each

## Performance
- **Maximum Speed:** 411 knots (473 mph, 761 km/h)
- **Cruise Speed:** 328 knots (377 mph, 607 km/h)
- **Range:** 2,380 nmi (2,739 mi, 4,408 km)
- **Endurance:** 16 hours
- **Service Ceiling:** 28,300 ft (8,626 m)

## Armament
- **Internal weapons bay:** 10 wing and fuselage stations
- **Weapons capacity:** Up to 20,000 lb (9,071 kg)

**Typical loadouts include:**
- **Torpedoes:** Mk 46, Mk 50, Mk 54 lightweight torpedoes
- **Anti-ship missiles:** AGM-84 Harpoon, AGM-65 Maverick
- **Depth charges:** Mk 101 Lulu nuclear depth charge (historical)
- **Mines:** Mk 62, Mk 63, and Mk 65 Quickstrike naval mines
- **Bombs:** Various general-purpose and cluster bombs

## Fleet Information
- **Inventory:** Approximately 14 in active service (as of 2025), down from peak of over 200
- **Unit Cost:** $36 million (original cost), not currently in production

## Deployment
The P-3C Orion is in the final stages of being phased out in favor of the P-8A Poseidon but remains deployed at limited locations.

### Primary Deployment Locations:
- **Naval Air Station Jacksonville, Florida**
  - VP-30 "Pro's Nest" (Training)
- **Naval Air Station Whidbey Island, Washington**
  - VQ-1 "World Watchers" (EP-3E variant)

### Historical Squadron Deployments (most now transitioned to P-8A):
- VP-1, VP-4, VP-5, VP-8, VP-9, VP-10, VP-16, VP-26, VP-40, VP-45, VP-46, VP-47

## Mission
The P-3C Orion served as the U.S. Navy's primary maritime patrol aircraft for over 50 years. Primary missions include:

- Anti-submarine warfare (ASW)
- Anti-surface warfare (ASuW)
- Maritime surveillance and reconnaissance
- Over-land intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR)
- Search and rescue (SAR)
- Communications intelligence (COMINT)
- Electronic intelligence (ELINT) (EP-3E variant)

## Features
- **Magnetic Anomaly Detector (MAD):** For submarine detection
- **Acoustic Systems:** Sonobuoy processor and dispensers
- **Radar:** AN/APS-115 multi-mode surface search radar
- **Electro-Optical/Infrared (EO/IR):** Forward-looking infrared (FLIR) on upgraded variants
- **Electronic Support Measures (ESM):** Various systems for electronic intelligence gathering
- **Mission Systems:** Tactical displays and mission computers
- **Communications:** HF, VHF, UHF, and SATCOM capabilities

## History
The P-3 Orion was developed from the Lockheed L-188 Electra commercial airliner as a replacement for the P-2 Neptune. The P-3C variant, introduced in 1969, featured significant avionics and weapons systems upgrades. For over five decades, the P-3C served as the backbone of the U.S. Navy's maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare capabilities during the Cold War and beyond. The aircraft played a crucial role in tracking Soviet submarines, conducting maritime surveillance, and later supporting operations in conflicts from Vietnam to the Global War on Terror. Starting in 2013, the P-3C began being replaced by the P-8A Poseidon, with the transition largely complete by 2023. The remaining P-3C aircraft are scheduled to be fully retired by 2026, ending more than 60 years of operational service.
