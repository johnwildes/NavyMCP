# USS John C. Stennis (CVN-74)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS John C. Stennis
- **Hull Number**: CVN-74
- **Commissioned**: December 9, 1995
- **Displacement**: Approximately 100,000 long tons (100,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,092 feet (332.8 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (40.8 m) waterline, 252 feet (76.8 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 37 feet (11.3 m)
- **Power Plant**: 2 A4W nuclear reactors, 4 steam turbines, 4 shafts, 260,000 shaft horsepower
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: Approximately 5,000 (Ship's company: 3,200, Air wing: 1,800)
- **Aircraft**: 60+ aircraft including F/A-18 Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS John C. Stennis (CVN-74) was commissioned on December 9, 1995, named after Senator John C. Stennis, who served as chairman of the Armed Services Committee and was a strong advocate for the U.S. Navy. The carrier has deployed extensively to the Western Pacific and Middle East, supporting Operations Southern Watch, Enduring Freedom, and Iraqi Freedom. In 2019, the Stennis conducted a significant deployment to the Mediterranean Sea and Middle East, participating in operations against ISIS. In early 2021, the carrier entered Newport News Shipbuilding to begin its mid-life Refueling and Complex Overhaul (RCOH), a four-year process that will refuel its nuclear reactors and significantly upgrade its combat systems and facilities. Throughout its service, the Stennis has demonstrated the strategic flexibility of carrier operations and served as a key instrument of U.S. foreign policy in regions of vital national interest.
