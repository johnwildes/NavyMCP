# USS Theodore Roosevelt (CVN-71)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS Theodore Roosevelt
- **Hull Number**: CVN-71
- **Commissioned**: October 25, 1986
- **Displacement**: Approximately 100,000 long tons (100,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,092 feet (332.8 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (40.8 m) waterline, 252 feet (76.8 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 37 feet (11.3 m)
- **Power Plant**: 2 A4W nuclear reactors, 4 steam turbines, 4 shafts, 260,000 shaft horsepower
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: Approximately 5,000 (Ship's company: 3,200, Air wing: 1,800)
- **Aircraft**: 60+ aircraft including F/A-18 Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS Theodore Roosevelt (CVN-71), nicknamed "The Big Stick," was commissioned on October 25, 1986, named after the 26th President of the United States. The carrier has been deployed extensively to both the Mediterranean Sea and the Persian Gulf, playing significant roles in Operation Desert Storm, Operation Allied Force in Kosovo, and Operation Iraqi Freedom. In 2020, the Roosevelt gained international attention when it experienced a COVID-19 outbreak while deployed in the Pacific, leading to the controversial relief of its commanding officer. Throughout its service, the Theodore Roosevelt has demonstrated the strategic flexibility of carrier operations, conducting combat missions, humanitarian assistance, and disaster response operations across the globe while serving as a vital component of America's forward naval presence.
