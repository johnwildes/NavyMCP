# USS George H.W. Bush (CVN-77)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS George H.W. Bush
- **Hull Number**: CVN-77
- **Commissioned**: January 10, 2009
- **Displacement**: Approximately 102,000 long tons (104,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,092 feet (332.8 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (40.8 m) waterline, 252 feet (76.8 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 37 feet (11.3 m)
- **Power Plant**: 2 A4W nuclear reactors, 4 steam turbines, 4 shafts, 260,000 shaft horsepower
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: Approximately 5,000 (Ship's company: 3,200, Air wing: 1,800)
- **Aircraft**: 60+ aircraft including F/A-18 Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS George H.W. Bush (CVN-77) is the tenth and final Nimitz-class aircraft carrier, commissioned on January 10, 2009, while its namesake, the 41st President of the United States, was still alive to witness the ceremony. As the transition ship between the Nimitz and Ford classes, it incorporates several design improvements including a bulbous bow, redesigned island structure, and updated electronics. The carrier has deployed multiple times to the Mediterranean Sea and Persian Gulf, playing a significant role in Operation Inherent Resolve against ISIS in 2017. In 2019, the Bush entered Norfolk Naval Shipyard for a 28-month maintenance period to upgrade systems and perform repairs. As the most technologically advanced of the Nimitz-class carriers, the George H.W. Bush continues to serve as a crucial element of American naval power projection while incorporating lessons that informed the development of the subsequent Ford-class carriers.
