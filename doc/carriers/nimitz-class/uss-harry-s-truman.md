# USS Harry S. Truman (CVN-75)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS Harry S. Truman
- **Hull Number**: CVN-75
- **Commissioned**: July 25, 1998
- **Displacement**: Approximately 100,000 long tons (100,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,092 feet (332.8 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (40.8 m) waterline, 252 feet (76.8 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 37 feet (11.3 m)
- **Power Plant**: 2 A4W nuclear reactors, 4 steam turbines, 4 shafts, 260,000 shaft horsepower
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: Approximately 5,000 (Ship's company: 3,200, Air wing: 1,800)
- **Aircraft**: 60+ aircraft including F/A-18 Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS Harry S. Truman (CVN-75) was commissioned on July 25, 1998, named after the 33rd President of the United States. The carrier has been extensively deployed to the Mediterranean Sea and the Persian Gulf, participating in Operations Southern Watch, Enduring Freedom, and Iraqi Freedom. In 2016, the Truman participated in Operation Inherent Resolve, conducting airstrikes against ISIS targets. In 2018, it became the first U.S. aircraft carrier to operate in the Arctic Circle in nearly 30 years, participating in NATO's Exercise Trident Juncture. During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, the carrier remained at sea longer than expected to maintain operational readiness while minimizing exposure risk. Throughout its service, the Truman has played a vital role in demonstrating American commitment to allies and partners while providing a flexible and powerful instrument of naval power projection across the globe.
