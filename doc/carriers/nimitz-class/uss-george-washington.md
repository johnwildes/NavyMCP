# USS George Washington (CVN-73)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS George Washington
- **Hull Number**: CVN-73
- **Commissioned**: July 4, 1992
- **Displacement**: Approximately 100,000 long tons (100,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,092 feet (332.8 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (40.8 m) waterline, 252 feet (76.8 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 37 feet (11.3 m)
- **Power Plant**: 2 A4W nuclear reactors, 4 steam turbines, 4 shafts, 260,000 shaft horsepower
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: Approximately 5,000 (Ship's company: 3,200, Air wing: 1,800)
- **Aircraft**: 60+ aircraft including F/A-18 Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS George Washington (CVN-73) was commissioned on July 4, 1992, symbolically celebrating American independence while honoring the first U.S. President. From 2008 to 2015, the carrier served as the U.S. Navy's forward-deployed carrier operating from Yokosuka, Japan, the first nuclear-powered surface warship permanently deployed in Japanese waters. During this period, it played a crucial role in disaster relief following the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami. The George Washington has participated in numerous operations in the Mediterranean, Persian Gulf, and Western Pacific, including Operations Southern Watch and Enduring Freedom. In 2021, the carrier began a complex Refueling and Complex Overhaul (RCOH) at Newport News Shipbuilding, expected to be completed by 2027, which will extend its operational lifespan by approximately 25 years and update its combat systems to meet future threats.
