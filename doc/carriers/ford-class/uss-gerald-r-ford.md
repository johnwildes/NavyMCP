# USS Gerald R. Ford (CVN-78)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS Gerald R. Ford
- **Hull Number**: CVN-78
- **Commissioned**: July 22, 2017
- **Displacement**: Approximately 100,000 long tons (100,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,106 feet (337 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (41 m) waterline, 256 feet (78 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 39 feet (12 m)
- **Power Plant**: Two A1B nuclear reactors, four shafts
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: 2,600 (ship only; approximately 4,400 with full air wing)
- **Aircraft**: 75+ aircraft including F-35C Lightning II, F/A-18E/F Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2D Advanced Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS Gerald R. Ford (CVN-78) is the lead ship of her class and represents the first new aircraft carrier design constructed for the United States Navy since USS Nimitz was commissioned in 1975. Named after the 38th President of the United States, the carrier was commissioned on July 22, 2017, following years of development and construction delays. The Ford features numerous technological advancements, including the Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System (EMALS), Advanced Arresting Gear (AAG), dual-band radar, and increased electrical power generation. After extensive post-delivery testing and trials to address various technical challenges, the Ford completed its first deployment in November 2022, conducting operations in the Atlantic Ocean and Mediterranean Sea. As the most advanced aircraft carrier in the world, the Gerald R. Ford represents the future of naval aviation and serves as the template for subsequent Ford-class carriers that will form the backbone of American naval power through the mid-21st century.
