# U.S. 1st Fleet

## Overview
The United States First Fleet is a numbered fleet of the United States Navy reestablished in 2020 to focus on the Indo-Pacific region, specifically the eastern portion of the U.S. Indo-Pacific Command area of responsibility. This reestablishment represents a strategic shift to enhance U.S. naval presence in a critical region.

## Area of Operations
- **Primary Region**: Indo-Pacific region
- **Coverage Area**: Eastern Indian Ocean through the Pacific Ocean
- **Strategic Focus**: Maintaining freedom of navigation, ensuring maritime security, and countering Chinese naval expansion
- **Strategic Importance**: Covers key maritime chokepoints and sea lanes vital to global trade

## Personnel
- **Active Duty**: ~300-500 personnel (headquarters)
- **Civilian Employees**: ~50-100
- **Total Personnel**: ~350-600
- **Note**: The First Fleet is still developing its operational structure and personnel requirements

## Headquarters
- **Location**: Initially planned for Singapore, but the final headquarters location is still under determination
- **Temporary Facility**: Currently operates within existing U.S. naval infrastructure in the region

## Command Structure
- Reports to U.S. Indo-Pacific Command
- Coordinates with U.S. 7th Fleet for regional operations
- Develops partnerships with regional allies including Australia, India, and Japan
- Operates as part of the broader U.S. strategy in the Indo-Pacific region

## Major Units and Assets
- No permanently assigned forces (still in development)
- Expected to have operational control of:
  - Surface action groups
  - Carrier strike group elements
  - Expeditionary strike groups
  - Submarine forces
  - Maritime patrol aircraft

## History
The original U.S. First Fleet was established during World War II and operated in the Western Pacific. After the war, it was eventually merged into other fleet structures. In December 2020, then-Navy Secretary Kenneth Braithwaite announced plans to reestablish the First Fleet to focus on the Indo-Pacific region, particularly the critical area around the Indian Ocean and Southeast Asia. This decision came amid growing Chinese naval influence in the region and represents a significant shift in the U.S. Navy's force posture in the Indo-Pacific. The reestablishment of the First Fleet is part of a larger Department of Defense strategy to maintain U.S. military superiority in the region through distributed maritime operations and strengthened alliances.

## Current Mission
The First Fleet's mission is to maintain maritime security, ensure freedom of navigation and overflight, and promote adherence to international rules and norms in the Indo-Pacific region. It is designed to operate in partnership with regional allies and partners to counter growing Chinese naval influence and ensure that critical sea lanes remain open for global commerce. Through persistent presence and operations, the First Fleet will advance strategic U.S. interests while reassuring allies of American commitment to regional stability.
