# U.S. 7th Fleet

## Overview
The United States Seventh Fleet is a numbered fleet of the United States Navy operating in the Western Pacific and Indian Ocean. It is the largest of the U.S. Navy's forward-deployed fleets, with 50-70 ships, 150 aircraft and approximately 20,000 sailors and Marines operating in the region at any given time.

## Area of Operations
- **Primary Region**: Western Pacific and Indian Ocean
- **Coverage Area**: More than 124 million square kilometers, stretching from the International Date Line to the India/Pakistan border and from the Kuril Islands in the North to the Antarctic in the South
- **Strategic Focus**: South China Sea, East China Sea, Sea of Japan, Philippine Sea, and Indian Ocean
- **Countries**: Operations adjacent to 36 maritime countries including China, Russia, North Korea, South Korea, Japan, Philippines, Vietnam, and Indonesia

## Personnel
- **Active Duty**: ~2,000-2,500 personnel (headquarters)
- **Civilian Employees**: ~200-300
- **Total Personnel**: ~2,200-2,800
- **Fleet Assets**: 50-70 ships, 150 aircraft, and approximately 20,000 sailors and Marines

## Headquarters
- **Location**: U.S. Fleet Activities Yokosuka, Japan
- **Facility**: Seventh Fleet Command Headquarters

## Command Structure
- Reports to Commander, U.S. Pacific Fleet
- Works closely with allies and partners in the Indo-Pacific region
- Forward-deployed command structure with permanent presence in Japan and periodic presence throughout the region
- Command extends over combined task forces and multiple component commands

## Major Units and Assets
- Flagship: USS Blue Ridge (LCC-19), command ship
- Carrier Strike Group 5 centered on USS Ronald Reagan (CVN-76)
- Task Force 70 (Battle Force 7th Fleet)
- Task Force 71 (Patrol and Reconnaissance Force)
- Task Force 72 (Logistics Force)
- Task Force 73 (Mine Warfare)
- Task Force 74 (Submarine Force)
- Task Force 75 (Expeditionary Forces)
- Task Force 76 (Amphibious Force)
- Task Force 77 (Surface Combatant Force)
- Task Force 78 (Service Force)
- Multiple Amphibious Ready Groups
- Multiple Destroyer Squadrons
- Forward Deployed Naval Forces in Japan and Guam

## History
The Seventh Fleet was formed during World War II on March 15, 1943, under the command of Admiral Arthur S. Carpender. It played a pivotal role in Pacific amphibious operations during the war. Following World War II, the fleet participated in the occupation of Japan and later provided naval support during the Korean War, including the famous Inchon Landing. Throughout the Cold War era, the Seventh Fleet was a critical component of U.S. power projection in Asia, particularly during the Vietnam War. After the fall of the Soviet Union, the fleet's mission evolved to focus on regional stability, crisis response, and defense of key allies like Japan and South Korea. In recent decades, the Seventh Fleet has responded to natural disasters like the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami and the 2011 Tohoku earthquake while also adapting to meet the challenges posed by China's growing maritime power and North Korea's nuclear ambitions.

## Current Mission
The Seventh Fleet conducts forward-deployed naval operations in support of U.S. national interests in the Indo-Pacific region. Its primary mission areas include ensuring maritime security and freedom of navigation, fostering and maintaining cooperative security relationships with allies and partners, responding to crises and natural disasters, deterring aggression, and maintaining combat readiness to win conflicts if deterrence fails.
