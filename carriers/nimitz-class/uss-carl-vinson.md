# USS Carl Vinson (CVN-70)

## Characteristics
- **Ship Name**: USS Carl Vinson
- **Hull Number**: CVN-70
- **Commissioned**: March 13, 1982
- **Displacement**: Approximately 100,000 long tons (100,000 t) full load
- **Length**: 1,092 feet (332.8 m)
- **Beam**: 134 feet (40.8 m) waterline, 252 feet (76.8 m) extreme
- **Draft**: 37 feet (11.3 m)
- **Power Plant**: 2 A4W nuclear reactors, 4 steam turbines, 4 shafts, 260,000 shaft horsepower
- **Speed**: 30+ knots (56+ km/h)
- **Crew Size**: Approximately 5,000 (Ship's company: 3,200, Air wing: 1,800)
- **Aircraft**: 60+ aircraft including F/A-18 Super Hornets, EA-18G Growlers, E-2 Hawkeyes, and helicopters

## History
The USS Carl Vinson (CVN-70) was commissioned on March 13, 1982, and named after Congressman Carl Vinson who served in the House for over 50 years. The carrier gained significant public attention when it was used to bury Osama bin Laden at sea following his death in 2011. The Carl Vinson has participated in numerous operations including Operation Southern Watch over Iraq, Operation Iraqi Freedom, and humanitarian efforts such as disaster relief in Haiti following the 2010 earthquake. In 2018, the carrier made a historic port call to Vietnam, the first by a U.S. aircraft carrier since the Vietnam War ended. Throughout its service, the Carl Vinson has been instrumental in projecting American naval power across the globe and maintaining stability in regions of strategic importance.
