# USS O'Kane (DDG-77)

**Class:** Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (Flight II)
**Namesake:** Rear Admiral Richard O'Kane
**Commissioned:** October 23, 1999
**Status:** In active service
**Homeport:** Naval Base San Diego, California

## Specifications
- **Displacement:** 8,900 tons (full load)
- **Length:** 505 ft (154 m)
- **Beam:** 66 ft (20 m)
- **Draft:** 31 ft (9.4 m)
- **Propulsion:** 4 General Electric LM2500 gas turbines, 2 shafts, 100,000 shp (75 MW)
- **Speed:** 30+ knots (56+ km/h; 35+ mph)
- **Complement:** 380 officers and enlisted

## Armament
- 1 × 5-inch (127 mm)/54 caliber Mk 45 gun

- 4 × .50-cal (12.7 mm) machine guns
- 1 × 20 mm Phalanx CIWS
- 1 × 32 cell, 1 × 64 cell Mk 41 vertical launch systems
- 90 × RIM-156 SM-2, BGM-109 Tomahawk, RUM-139 VL-ASROC
- 8 × RGM-84 Harpoon missiles
- 2 × Mk 32 triple torpedo tubes
- 6 × Mk 46 or Mk 50 torpedoes

## Aircraft
- 1 × SH-60 LAMPS III helicopter

## History
USS O'Kane is the twenty-seventh ship of the Arleigh Burke class of guided missile destroyers and the first Flight II variant. She is named for Rear Admiral Richard O'Kane, a submarine commander who received the Medal of Honor for his actions during World War II. As commanding officer of USS Tang, O'Kane was credited with sinking 24 Japanese ships during his five patrols, more than any other American submarine commander.

The ship was commissioned on October 23, 1999, and was formerly homeported at Naval Station Pearl Harbor, Hawaii before being reassigned to San Diego. USS O'Kane has participated in numerous operations and is equipped with the Aegis Combat System for ballistic missile defense.
